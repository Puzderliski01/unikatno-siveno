The honest fine print — all optional, all off by default, and early

All of this is optional. Every AI feature ships switched off, and Charmling is a complete app without any of them — the charm, the physics, the rituals, Wander Mode, the focus timer and the basket all work on their own and never read your screen. Turn the AI on if you want it, turn it off whenever, and it stops the same second.

And it's early. A model small enough to run on your own Mac is a model with limits: it writes a good first draft and it will sometimes miss the point of an hour. Treat every recap, standup and timesheet as a draft to read before you send it. It's the part of Charmling I'm working on hardest, and it gets better with every release — the on-device models are improving quickly too, and Charmling picks that up for free. Everything here needs Apple Intelligence: Apple silicon, macOS 26+.

Live scores · beta · the one part that needs the internet

Your match turns, the cord jolts. Nothing about you moves.

A small brass tag drops below the charm you already have, with the score on it — or, if you would rather, a split-flap departure board takes the cord instead. A wicket falls, or a car takes P1, and the cord jolts: a bell sounds once and the board flips to the moment. Twelve sports, cricket first. Scores have to come from somewhere, so this is the one part of Charmling that reaches the internet: it asks one fixed address for the same public scoreboard everyone gets, and tells it nothing about you.

  1. Switch it on. Control-click the charm and tick 🔔 Live Scores (needs internet). It is off out of the box, and it tells you exactly what it will fetch before it fetches anything.
  2. Choose your sports. Sports to follow lists all twelve, cricket first. Then open the board and tap a match to star the teams in it — following is a tap, not a form.
  3. Say where it shows. Where the score shows is two lines: on the charm you are wearing, or on a scoreboard of its own. Switch back and your charm comes straight back — the board never costs you the one you chose. How both work →

The honest list — what you're not getting

None of the screen-reading is on until you switch it on — and the same switch stops it that second. Charmling is a whole app without it.

  • No endless trial. Seven free days are built in: the whole app, no card, no account. After that the charm sleeps until a licence wakes it. The seven-day refund sits on top of that week, not instead of it.
  • Mac only. No Windows, no iPhone, no web version, and none planned soon.
  • One Mac, and nothing syncs. Your charms, your Garden and your memory live on that machine and go nowhere else, not even to your other Mac. It is the same decision as "no cloud", seen from the inconvenient side. Changing machines is free: write to me and I'll move the licence.
  • No AI without Apple Intelligence. On an Intel Mac, or an M-series Mac without it switched on, you get the charm, the physics, the 99 charms, the Garden, Wander Mode, the timer and the basket. You get none of Day Memory, recaps, standups, timesheets, quizzes or Meeting Radar. That is most of the price. Buy on that basis.
  • macOS will show the recording indicator in your menu bar whenever Charmling does look at the screen as a picture. Most apps hand over their text instead, so you will see it less than you used to, but you will see it. The system can't be stopped from showing it, and you'd be right to distrust anything that hid it.
  • What it writes is a draft. A model small enough to live on your Mac produces a solid first pass and will sometimes miss the point of an hour. Read a timesheet before you hand it to anyone.
  • The redaction is careful, not perfect. No content filter is. That is why the log lives 48 hours, sits in one plain file you can read, and erases in one click. All of it, the last hour, or everything from one app.
  • It's one person. Support is as fast as one person can be, which is usually the same day and occasionally not. There is no queue, no tier and no bot. Found a security problem? Put "security" in the subject and it jumps the queue — the privacy page says what to expect.
  • If it's ever discontinued, a build that needs no licence check gets published, so what you bought keeps working. That's in the licence terms, not just here.

Questions, answered

Seven things people ask before they buy.

Answers open, because a wall of closed boxes is a way of not answering.

Is there a subscription?

No. One payment, and every update after it is included. There is nothing to subscribe to: the AI is Apple’s own model, already on your Mac, so there are no servers and no monthly bills to pass on to you. $14.99₹499$7.99, once.

Does anything from my screen leave my Mac?

No. What it reads is turned into text on your Mac, and that log erases itself after 48 hours. The app makes five kinds of internet request in total: an update check, activating your licence and re‑checking it weekly, downloading an update when you ask, and the public scoreboard if you switch Live Scores on. None of them carry anything from your screen. The complete accounting →

Do I have to use the AI?

No, and it is off the day you install it. The charm, its physics, Wander Mode, the timer and the basket never read your screen or ask a model anything. One toggle silences every capture at once. Be a little sceptical of what it writes, too — a model small enough to live on your Mac gives you a good first draft, not a finished one.

Could it capture my passwords?

It is built so it doesn’t. Password managers are skipped before a screenshot is taken, including one open as a web page. Anything shaped like a secret — a token, a key, a card number, a masked field — is deleted before a word is written down, in nine languages. No filter is perfect, which is why the log lives 48 hours in one plain file you can open and read, and one click erases it. Watch it run on a real screen →

Can I use it on a work Mac?

Technically yes, and check first. Stay Active keeps you showing as available in Teams and Slack, and some employers treat that as misconduct — Wells Fargo dismissed more than a dozen people over simulated activity. It is off until you switch it on, and it is the one I would think hardest about on a company machine. Your IT department can also see that you granted a permission, though never what was done with it.

What does it need to run?

The charm, the physics, Wander Mode, the timer and the basket need macOS 13 or later. The AI features need Apple Intelligence, which means an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 26 with it switched on. It asks for four permissions, all optional, all explained in plain language before macOS asks — decline every one and the charm still hangs there and works. Which permission does what →

What if it’s not for me?

The first seven days are free and built in — the whole app, no card, no account. If you buy and change your mind anyway, reply to your receipt or write to support@charmling.app within seven days of buying and it is refunded. No form, no questions.
